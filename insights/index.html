How guests really rate luxury hotels

What 1,341 luxury hotels look like when you put real guest reviews next to the critics, the awards, the price and the destination. Every chart uses hotels with a guest score from 20 or more reviews, and every rank is among the hotels on StayOdds.

Guests versus awards

Award winners guests don't love

Award-winning hotels that rank lowest with guests in their own country. Each is ranked by guest score against the other hotels in that country on StayOdds (only countries with 10 or more hotels). Left is guests' best, right is guests' worst.

Luxury guest scores sit close together (most are between 88 and 98), so a hotel can rank last in its country on a score that is still good. That is why the score and the number of reviews are shown under every rank.
Hotel brands

How guests rate each luxury brand

Dot = the brand's typical (median) hotel. Thick bar = the middle half of its hotels. Thin line = best to worst hotel. A short bar means a consistent brand. Brands with 8 or more rated hotels.

Most consistent: The Peninsula, where every rated hotel scores between 89 and 94. Widest spread: One&Only, from 72 to 96. Hover over or tap a brand to see its best and lowest-rated hotel. Brand logos: Wikimedia Commons, Wikipedia, Logopedia and the brands' own websites.
Critics versus guests

Where critics and guests disagree

Every hotel with both a published critic score and a guest score (659 hotels). Above the line, guests like it more than critics; below it, critics like it more. Orange dots are the six biggest "critics loved it, guests less so" gaps; hover any dot for its name, click to open its page.

Critic scores are the published scores linked on each hotel page, converted to a score out of 100.
Awards

Do awards match guest opinion?

Typical guest score by award. Dot = median hotel, bar = middle half.

Award-winning hotels have a typical guest score of 94 to 94, against 92 for hotels with no major award. Awards point to good hotels, but guests separate them far less than the lists do.
Price and value

Does paying more buy happier guests?

Guest score by price level. Price level comes from our latest nightly price check for each hotel (a sample date, so treat it as a guide). Dot = typical hotel, thick bar = middle half, thin line = best to worst.

Typical guest score rises from 93 (under £300) to 94 (£1,000+), but every price level has hotels guests rate 98 or more, so a higher price is no guarantee.
